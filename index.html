The Problem

Your current system has no memory.

Jails run on institutional memory, and institutional memory walks out the door at every shift change.

Paper disappears

Passdowns, property sheets, warrant refusals, restraint logs. The record of what happened lives on a clipboard until it doesn't.

Deadlines are invisible

First-appearance windows, detainer clocks, check intervals, restraint limits. They get missed, not from negligence, but because nothing in the building is counting.

Nobody knows who has whom

A person leaves the floor for court, medical, a work detail, a hospital. The question of who has them right now gets answered by radio, if at all.

Command finds out after

The relieving sergeant pieces the night together from nineteen screens and a conversation. The Sheriff hears about it from a lawyer.

We built Argus because we lived these problems. Not as consultants. As the sergeant on the floor at 2 a.m., trying to find a warrant nobody could locate.

The Platform

One system. Booking to release.

Argus covers the whole custody lifecycle on the web and on a rugged handheld the officer carries. Every module writes to the same record, under the same rules, with the same name on every line.

Intake & Booking

One true start. Identity, charges against the Georgia code, nurse screening, property, photographs that pass the standard.

Housing & Custody

Classification, keep-separates, unit states, and a custody model that always knows who holds a person and where.

Movement & Scanning

Tap a wall tag, scan a wristband: rounds, headcounts, moves and escorts recorded on the handheld in the time it takes to walk.

Court & First Appearance

The docket inside the constitutional deadline, bail orders with their judge, bond conditions the release desk can read.

Sentence & Release

One projected release date across every sentence, every hold surfaced, and nothing releases by itself.

Medical & Mental Health

Sick call, watch placements with their check clocks, and proof that a person was seen - by whom, and when.

External Transports & Off-Site

Court pulls, hospital posts, admissions, reliefs and check-ins. Custody that leaves the building without leaving the record.

Command

The Tower's live picture, notifications that reach the right rank, and a shift passdown written all day and signed at handover.

Why Argus

Built different, on purpose.

Legacy jail software was written for records clerks. Argus was written for the people on the floor at three in the morning, and for the command staff who answer for them.

01
Written by the people who run the floor

A working Senior Sergeant and a 1st Lieutenant with eighteen years in jail administration. Every workflow in Argus was worked on a real shift before it was coded, and the rulings that shape it are recorded by name.

02
Attribution, not "the system"

Argus refuses to record an act without the officer's own credential behind it. Reports print who wrote them, who edited them, and whether a template or an assistant helped. That is what a court will ask.

03
The clocks are the product

Suicide-watch checks, restraint ceilings, detainer windows, first-appearance deadlines, off-site check-ins. Each one is counted from the moment it starts and surfaced to the rank that can act. A missed check is a permanent fact, not a warning that clears itself.

04
The handheld is the officer's, not the vendor's

A rugged device with NFC. One scan and one tap for the act itself. Authenticates on your network. Keeps working when the internet doesn't. Records custody hand-offs in a hospital corridor at 0100.

05
Shift change in one screen

The Commander's Passdown, composed from the record. Counts, who is out of the building, what will bite the next watch. Every line marked as read from the system or typed by a named officer. Signed by the Watch Commander, reprinted byte-for-byte.

06
Driven end to end before you see it

We don't demo slides. We run real days against the software: a fight in the dayroom, a hospital run, bond day, shift change. We fix what those days break before a pilot begins, and you will see the record of that, not a promise.

How it works

Every step. Every person.

01
Intake & Booking

The arresting officer's sheet is one true start. Identity is resolved against the master person index, charges are entered against the Georgia code, the nurse's gate is honored before housing, and the photographs meet the standard the state will accept.

02
Classification & Housing

Argus drafts the classification from charges, history, flags and keep-separates. A sergeant rules on it. Housing enforces the rulings, and a move on the floor is a scan, not a form.

03
Custody, all day

Every movement, contact, check, hand-off and off-site leg flows to one record. The Tower sees the building live. The passdown writes itself as the day happens, and the relieving sergeant reads one screen instead of nineteen.

04
Release with the whole picture

Every hold, warrant, bond condition, detainer clock and sentence projection is on the release desk's screen before the door opens. The decision is always a human's. The picture is always complete.
